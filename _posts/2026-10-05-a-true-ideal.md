I used to think I had an ideal. Gradually I found out that I did not.

What in this world deserves to be called an ideal? My so-called ideals of the past were nothing more than hopes of gaining wealth and fame through them.

Do I want to be a writer? I'm afraid not that much. I am already 35 and have never done much serious writing. Other pursuits I am almost too embarrassed to mention. I tried a few as a child, but I had neither formal training nor a heaven-sent gift. So if I am to talk about ideals today, I have basically accomplished nothing.

Of course, I am quite satisfied with my life and full of expectations. Without much effort I obtained law licenses in two countries, and if my visa goes smoothly, next year I will head to Seattle to start a new future. I have a happy family and two lovely children. Whatever modest talents I have are probably reflected in these two places for now.

Perhaps a happy life, and creating good conditions for one's family and the next generation, is also one of life's ideals. I have done well at it, and I am proud of it.

I have been in a low mood lately, so I keep listening to Beyond's songs. On top of that, Yip Sai-wing has been touring the mainland recently, so his videos are everywhere in my feeds. I miss Wong Ka Kui so much.

He died on June 30, 1993. I was about two years old then. To think that for the first two years of my life, Ka Kui and I existed in this world together gives me a strange sense of fulfillment.

I started listening to his songs around 2003. A middle school classmate recommended Beyond to me. That also happened to be the year of the 2003 Beyond concert. So for several years after that, through tapes, CDs and the internet, I watched the 1991 concert, the 2003 concert, and a large share of Beyond's popular songs again and again. I hardly listened to anything else. Jay Chou and the like, I rarely listened to. Perhaps that also shows I am not very interested in pop music. Then at 17 I taught myself guitar, and I can manage basic playing and singing. At that age one does unconventional things and imagines having many ideals. But all in all, everyone's world is a self-contained whole, and Beyond has always been part of the basic structure of mine.

Time flies. In a blink, Ka Kui has been gone for 33 years. I am 35. Forget achieving an ideal; I no longer even know what my ideal is. Maybe my ideal is just to make a lot of money.

But I am sure there are still things I want to explore. Money lets you explore the world, in the material sense. As for the spiritual world, if I want to keep exploring it, I can only place my hopes in the art of words. Perhaps in my mind, only art truly deserves to be called an ideal. Otherwise, as a mortal of flesh and blood, apart from artistic creation in the broadest sense, what else could be called an ideal? Everything else is a game of competing for resources. Competing for resources should not be called an ideal.

Oh right, world peace is also an ideal. That is the ultimate ideal.

Perhaps letting yourself live a good life does not count as an ideal, but letting more people live a good life is an ideal of a kind. Whether it is letting your family live well through your own abilities, or, as an entrepreneur, letting people live better through your products or services, these are ideals worthy of respect. The more people live good lives, the greater the ideal. Bringing peace to the world is, of course, the ultimate ideal.

Exploring truth is also an ideal. Mathematics, physics, biology, the universe, philosophy. Expanding the boundaries of human knowledge, even if the final aim is not to turn it into a better standard of living, is a very pure ideal, because it represents the summit of human civilization.

So I can probably define an ideal now. It is what I once said as a joke: if one day aliens came to Earth and asked us what our ideals are, the ones we could say out loud at that moment are the true ideals.

If aliens took me away and asked what music I like, I would recommend Beyond's songs without hesitation, and recommend "Boundless Oceans, Vast Skies."

If the aliens asked what my ideal was and I said to become king of humankind, that would be deeply shameful; the aliens might simply wipe me out for being so vulgar. But if I said I want to create great works of art, if I said I want to understand the nature of the universe, the aliens would understand me.

I want to pick up a true ideal again. I still don't know what my ideal is, and I probably cannot make any direct contribution to peace for humankind. The only thing I have mastered is the Chinese written word. Maybe I should try writing. As a person of the material world, I command too few resources to really do anything. But before art, or rather before the creation of words, everyone is equal. Perhaps I really could add a brick to human civilization, a brick of a unique color, a brick with my name on it. That is not completely impossible.

All right. From today on, I am once again a person with an ideal. Thank you, Ka Kui, for the inspiration. You really are a remarkable person.
